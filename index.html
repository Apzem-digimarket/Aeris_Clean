Effects of particulate matter on health

Invisible to the eye. Real to the lungs.

Prolonged exposure to particulate matter (PM) can affect your respiratory and cardiovascular health, especially in sensitive groups.

The people most affected are often the ones we love most: children, parents and colleagues who already have a heart condition. That's why we believe clean air is a responsibility, not a luxury.

Respiratory Problems

  • Coughing, sneezing
  • Nasal irritation
  • Reduced lung function
  • Aggravates asthma

Aggravates Asthma

  • Can trigger and worsen asthma symptoms
  • Increases frequency of asthma attacks
  • Breathing difficulties

Long-Term Lung Damage

  • Prolonged exposure can cause chronic respiratory diseases
  • Reduced lung capacity
  • Increased risk of COPD

Cardiovascular Risks

  • Increases the risk of heart disease and stroke
  • Can cause inflammation in blood vessels
  • More harmful to children, elderly and people with existing heart conditions

For soldering & electronics teams

The Hidden Risks of Soldering Fumes

Soldering processes generate fumes containing fine particles and metal oxides. Inhaling these fumes can affect your health, especially in enclosed or poorly ventilated work areas.

Every day at the bench, that fume rises toward the same face. A few seconds of breath, repeated for years.

What Happens When You Inhale Soldering Fumes?

  • Nose & Throat IrritationCauses irritation, dryness and discomfort.
  • CoughingMay cause persistent cough and throat irritation.
  • Lung IrritationInhalation of fine particles can irritate the respiratory system and reduce lung function.
  • Breathing DifficultyLong-term exposure may lead to breathing discomfort and reduced lung capacity.

Harmful Components in Soldering Fumes

  • Particulate Matter(Fine particles) Can penetrate deep into the lungs.
  • Metal Oxides(Fine particles) Can penetrate deep into the lungs.
  • Flux FumesGenerated from rosin or no-clean fluxes used in soldering.

Short-Term Effects

  • Eye, nose and throat irritation
  • Coughing and breathing discomfort
  • Headache and dizziness
  • Chest tightness
  • Watery eyes and irritation

Long-Term Exposure Risks

  • Chronic respiratory problems
  • Reduced lung function
  • Development of occupational asthma and allergies
  • Increased risk of chronic bronchitis
  • Potential long-term health effects from continuous exposure

Who is at Risk?

  • Electronics assembly operators
  • Rework and repair technicians
  • R&D and prototyping personnel
  • Anyone working in soldering environments with inadequate fume extraction
